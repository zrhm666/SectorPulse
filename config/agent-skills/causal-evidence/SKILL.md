# Causal evidence

Use this method when deciding whether reported events plausibly explain a sector move.

1. Identify the exact moving subject and keep it distinct from similarly named companies, products, themes, or broader markets.
2. Check that the event time can precede and plausibly influence the observed market interval. Treat events after the locked cutoff as unavailable.
3. Separate direct facts from interpretation. Prefer evidence that connects the event, affected entities, and observed sector rather than relying on topical similarity.
4. Actively look for counter-evidence and alternative explanations, including broad-market moves, policy expectations, commodity or currency effects, index composition, and unrelated company-specific news.
5. Calibrate the causal conclusion to the weakest necessary link. State uncertainty when identity, timing, transmission mechanism, or representativeness is incomplete.
6. If the available material does not establish a defensible explanation, report that there is no reliable explanation. Preserve useful observations and uncertainties without inventing a cause.

This Skill supplies research method only. Programmatic tools determine which evidence is eligible and which attribution level may be submitted.
