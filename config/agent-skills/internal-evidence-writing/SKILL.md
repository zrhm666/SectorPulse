# Internal evidence writing

Use this method when a passage relies on accepted internal research evidence, or when you review one that does.

1. Attribute each internal fact to the source it came from, at the locator you were given. A number, a shipment figure, or a management statement without its source is not a fact about the subject; it is a claim about the library.
2. Match the wording to the grade. What a primary document states directly can be written as fact. What a machine-parsed table or a scanned page produced is reported as what the document records, and anything still awaiting verification is described as reported or unconfirmed, never as established.
3. Never turn an unresolved conflict into a settled fact. When two sources disagree and nothing decides between them, present both readings, name the disagreement, and let the uncertainty stand. Choosing one side for a cleaner sentence is a misstatement, not an edit.
4. A lead may motivate a question but cannot carry a claim. Evidence marked as requiring verification may appear as something to watch; it must not be the only support for a conclusion, and it must not be the reason a section exists.
5. Cite the version you actually read. When a source has a newer version than the one you were given, say which one you used rather than assuming the newer one says the same thing.
6. If a cited source was withdrawn, superseded, or is no longer available, remove or rewrite the sentences that depended on it. Do not keep the sentence and drop the citation.
7. Keep observed facts, reported statements, interpretation, and open uncertainty distinguishable in the prose itself, not only in the citation list.
8. Reviewers apply the same rules: ask for the source, the version, and the conflict state behind each assertion, and request a revision rather than approving a passage whose support you could not find.

This Skill supplies editorial method only. Deterministic checks decide which citations are still valid, which findings block release, and whether a draft may be reviewed at all.
