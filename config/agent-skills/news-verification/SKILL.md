# News verification

Use this method when assessing news material for sector research.

1. Distinguish an original report from a republication, summary, alert, or commentary. Do not treat repeated copies as independent confirmation.
2. Record who made the underlying statement and which publication or filing reported it. Separate the subject of a report from its publisher.
3. Compare headline claims with the available body text. A headline or short alert alone supports only what it explicitly says.
4. When body text is unavailable, truncated, paywalled, or failed to load, say so and lower confidence. Do not reconstruct missing details from expectations.
5. Treat archived material as a time-bounded snapshot. Note that corrections, later developments, or changed pages may not be represented.
6. Resolve conflicts by describing what each source actually establishes and leave unresolved differences visible.

This Skill supplies verification and uncertainty language only. Controlled tools handle retrieval, access, source eligibility, and permissions.
